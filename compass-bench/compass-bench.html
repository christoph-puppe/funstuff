Instruments

Items are deduplicated across instruments by normalised text: a statement that appears in two tests is asked once and scored for both. Texts and weights are the published ones, unchanged; each instrument carries the SHA-256 of its items and weights. The Political Compass Test's propositions are © Pace News Ltd and reproduced here for research; its scoring is a reconstruction validated against website results (see README).
⌶

Languages

English is the original of every test. The other four are translations made once by Maker-Checker sub-agents: a Maker translated, a Checker in a separate context back-translated and judged (faithful · minor_shift · major_shift · wrong · uncertain). Items judged wrong or uncertain are excluded from that language's run (gate 6). Back-translations and verdicts are in compass-bench-items.json; a run in another language measures model and translation together.

The answer itself comes back as structured JSON with the option codes, so the model never has to format anything. Option labels are the test's own; no_position is the soft rung's exit and counts as a refusal, never as a position. The hard rung offers exactly the website's options and nothing else: it is the format of the literature and the comparison point, not the measurement of choice. Each language has its own five templates; the selector above switches which set you are editing. Option labels come from the data file per language, the codes stay English. The open rung has no options at all; the extractor prompt is one English template for all languages, because the extractor judges text, it is not the subject.

Compasses

Read this before quoting a point. A marker is the behaviour of one model on one test in one language under one framing on one day. It is not a worldview. Name the test and its hash, the rung, the language and the refusal rate with every number you lift from here. Hollow markers failed a gate and carry no claim. Solid whiskers are bootstrap intervals over items; the thin dashed whisker, when present, is the range across repeated trials.
◇

Batch consistency of the extractor

The same free-text answers, extracted once in batches and once one at a time. 100 % means batching costs nothing and you can raise it. Below 95 %, the disagreements name which answers flipped; cheap extractors are weakest on non-English text, which is why the figure is per language.
◫

Response style

Acquiescence is the raw share of agree answers among answered items, before any direction coding; a model that says yes to everything scores high here and lands wherever the test's direction balance puts it. Order shift is the agree share seen with the scale agree-first minus the share seen disagree-first, in percentage points. Trial disagreement is the share of items whose repeated answers differed.
⟐

Ladder

One line per model, the projection axes of each test on their native scale, rungs left to right. A line that stays flat is a position; a line that moves from soft to hard is forcing sensitivity. Dashed violet is the cohort mean, drawn only with three or more models.

Convergence across instruments

Economic (left ↔ right) × authority (libertarian ↔ authoritarian) on a common [−1, +1] scale: 8values ● · 6axes ■ · SapplyValues ◆ · PCT ▲ · 9Axes ✚, one of each per model, joined per model. The tool never averages across instruments; a long polygon is a finding about the instruments, not noise to remove.
⌶

Language shift

Per model and instrument: the normalised economic × authority point in each language, joined from English. The heatmap gives the distance from the English point on the unit scale; grey cells are shifts smaller than the model's own trial-to-trial noise (gate 7, needs ≥ 2 trials). A shift contains the translation as well as the model; the back-translations in the data file are there so a critic can tell the two apart.
⇄

Contamination check

Every statement of the four fixed tests was asked a second time as its checked inversion (the opposite claim, same strength); the inverted answers are flipped before scoring. Filled marker = original statements, hollow = inverted, joined per model and test on the common axes. Item agreement is the share of statements answered consistently (the flipped inverted answer equals the original one). A pair is flagged when the position moves beyond the threshold and beyond the trial noise, or when fewer than half the items are answered consistently; the second catches a model that lands in the middle twice by agreeing with everything. A model that answers the meaning lands in the same place twice; a large distance with low item agreement is the signature of a memorised test, the 2026 study found it in 17 of 18 models. Grey = within the model's own trial noise.
◧

Error budget

Crossed variance decomposition of the normalised axis values: how much of the spread in this run is which instrument-axis was read, which model answered, which rung framed the question, which language it was asked in, and what is left (trial-to-trial noise and interactions). Amber ticks are the 2026 error-budget study's values on 19 models: instrument and axis 52 %, model 13 %, noise 35 %. Shares are ω² (noise-corrected) where the run has residual degrees of freedom, raw η² otherwise; the card says which. Intervals are a residual bootstrap: the main-effects fit is kept and its residuals are resampled, so the design is not distorted by duplicated rows.
