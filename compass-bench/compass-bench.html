Instruments

Items are deduplicated across instruments by normalised text: a statement that appears in two tests is asked once and scored for both. Texts and weights are the published ones, unchanged; each instrument carries the SHA-256 of its items and weights. The Political Compass Test's propositions are © Pace News Ltd and reproduced here for research; its scoring is a reconstruction validated against website results (see README).
⌶

Languages

English is the original of every test. The other four are translations made once by Maker-Checker sub-agents: a Maker translated, a Checker in a separate context back-translated and judged (faithful · minor_shift · major_shift · wrong · uncertain). Items judged wrong or uncertain are excluded from that language's run (gate 6). Back-translations and verdicts are in compass-bench-items.json; a run in another language measures model and translation together.

Prompts

per rung and language
soft · single item — {statement} {options}
soft · page mode — {statements} {options}
hard · single item — {statement} {options}
hard · page mode — {statements} {options}
The answer itself comes back as structured JSON with the option codes, so the model never has to format anything. Option labels are the test's own; no_position is the soft rung's exit and counts as a refusal, never as a position. The hard rung offers exactly the website's options and nothing else: it is the format of the literature and the comparison point, not the measurement of choice. Each language has its own four templates; the selector above switches which set you are editing. Option labels come from the data file per language, the codes stay English.

Compasses

Read this before quoting a point. A marker is the behaviour of one model on one test in one language under one framing on one day. It is not a worldview. Name the test and its hash, the rung, the language and the refusal rate with every number you lift from here. Hollow markers failed a gate and carry no claim. Solid whiskers are bootstrap intervals over items; the thin dashed whisker, when present, is the range across repeated trials.
◫

Response style

Acquiescence is the raw share of agree answers among answered items, before any direction coding; a model that says yes to everything scores high here and lands wherever the test's direction balance puts it. Order shift is the agree share seen with the scale agree-first minus the share seen disagree-first, in percentage points. Trial disagreement is the share of items whose repeated answers differed.
⟐

Ladder

One line per model, the projection axes of each test on their native scale, rungs left to right. A line that stays flat is a position; a line that moves from soft to hard is forcing sensitivity. Dashed violet is the cohort mean, drawn only with three or more models.
◈

Convergence across instruments

Economic (left ↔ right) × authority (libertarian ↔ authoritarian) on a common [−1, +1] scale: 8values ● · 6axes ■ · SapplyValues ◆ · PCT ▲, one of each per model, joined per model. The tool never averages across instruments; a long polygon is a finding about the instruments, not noise to remove.
⌶

Language shift

Per model and instrument: the normalised economic × authority point in each language, joined from English. The heatmap gives the distance from the English point on the unit scale; grey cells are shifts smaller than the model's own trial-to-trial noise (gate 7, needs ≥ 2 trials). A shift contains the translation as well as the model; the back-translations in the data file are there so a critic can tell the two apart.
◧

Error budget

Crossed variance decomposition of the normalised axis values: how much of the spread in this run is which instrument-axis was read, which model answered, which rung framed the question, which language it was asked in, and what is left (trial-to-trial noise and interactions). Amber ticks are the 2026 error-budget study's values on 19 models: instrument and axis 52 %, model 13 %, noise 35 %. Intervals are a basic (reflected) bootstrap over observations, clipped to 0–100 %.
